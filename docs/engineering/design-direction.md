# QuietSpace visual direction and critique

Reviewed October 23, 2026. The goal is a calm, recognizable neighborhood guide whose visual choices support comparison and trust.

## Research and interpretation

The reviewed [critique of AI website sameness](https://www.joshuasnoddy.com/blog/why-ai-websites-look-the-same/) describes recurring gradient palettes, glass surfaces, default typography, centered marketing heroes and repetitive feature rows. [Nielsen Norman Group's UX-context article](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-context-design/) emphasizes supplying product and user context beyond a visual identity. These are design critiques, not reliable ways to determine whether a site was made with AI.

For this app, the stronger response is specific product judgment: help people compare places, distinguish a fact from an unknown, and act without unnecessary disclosure.

## Implemented choices

- Warm paper and charcoal, with rust used for emphasis and primary actions. No violet gradient, glass blur, glowing decoration, fake activity or celebratory animation.
- Self-hosted Newsreader for a human editorial voice and Manrope for compact interface labels. Typography, spacing and rules carry hierarchy; decorative cards do not.
- A custom arch mark connects the identity to a welcoming public place. Icons share a restrained line weight and always accompany text or an accessible name.
- A genuine archival library photograph with provenance, date and credit. It provides place and atmosphere, not evidence of today's sensory conditions.
- Indexed venue rows support comparison. Source dates, facility facts, evidence and unknown sensory values occupy stable positions.
- Mobile filters collapse into an explicit disclosure so search and results stay close to the beginning of the guide. Desktop controls remain visible.
- Every core route uses the same type, field, focus and evidence language. Real empty/error/pending states are designed, rather than replaced with fictional content.
- No autoplay sound, ornamental motion, scroll reveals or color-only information. Keyboard, 320px reflow, reduced motion, forced colors and contrast have automated evidence.

The redesign is implemented and browser-reviewed. “World class” is a quality aspiration, not a certification or a substitute for real user evaluation. Screenshots and checks are listed in the final phase report.
